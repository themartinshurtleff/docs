# Liquidation Prediction Heatmap

This is the core of TradeNet Terminal. A proprietary self-calibrating liquidation prediction algorithm rendered as a real-time heatmap.

## What it does

The heatmap maps where forced liquidations will occur before the market gets there. This is not a static model. It recalibrates continuously as the market moves.

Other platforms use static liquidation models. They assume a fixed leverage distribution, estimate where liquidations sit, and never check those assumptions against reality. TradeNet's algorithm closes that loop. It uses real forced-liquidation events from the exchanges as feedback and adjusts its leverage weighting to match what traders are actually doing right now. You see the clusters forming before the sweep happens.

## How it works

The algorithm runs three steps continuously:

1. **Infer position entries.** Changes in open interest combined with taker aggression reveal where new leveraged positions opened and in which direction.
2. **Project liquidation prices.** Each inferred entry is projected across the leverage tiers traders actually use. BTC models tiers from 5x up to 250x. ETH goes up to 125x. SOL up to 75x.
3. **Calibrate against reality.** Real forced-liquidation events from Binance, Bybit, and OKX, normalized into one stream, feed back into the model. The leverage weighting shifts toward what the market is actually running.

Currently supported symbols: **BTC**, **ETH**, and **SOL**.

When price approaches a liquidation cluster, you know what is coming. A cascade of forced orders that will push price through that level. You position accordingly.

## What you see

An intensity grid over price and time. Long liquidation clusters sit below price. Short clusters sit above. Brighter means more projected liquidation volume at that level. Hover any cell for exact values.

The live snapshot updates roughly every 5 seconds. History is stored at 1-minute resolution, with about 12 hours hot and a growing long-term archive behind it.

## Zone lifecycle

Liquidation zones are not static pixels. Every zone moves through a lifecycle:

- **Created** when the model first projects liquidations at a level.
- **Reinforced** when more positions stack at the same level. The zone gets brighter.
- **Swept** when price trades through it. Swept zones fade rather than vanish instantly. A single wick does not erase a level that built up over hours.
- **Expired** when a zone sits untouched for hours and the positions behind it have likely closed or moved.

This is how you tell which levels have already been cleared and which are still loaded.

## Validated in live markets

On February 28, during the Iran strike, the algorithm identified exact liquidation clusters hours before 2,623 force orders swept through them. Traders watching the heatmap saw the setup forming well before the cascade hit. That is the difference between predicting and reacting.
