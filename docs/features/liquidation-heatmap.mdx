# Liquidation Prediction Heatmap

This is the core of TradeNet Terminal. A proprietary self-calibrating liquidation prediction algorithm rendered as a real-time heatmap.

## What it does

The heatmap maps where forced liquidations will occur before the market gets there. This is not a static model. It recalibrates continuously as the market moves.

Other platforms use static liquidation models. They assume a fixed leverage distribution, estimate where liquidations sit, and never check those assumptions against reality. TradeNet's algorithm closes that loop. It uses real forced-liquidation events from the exchanges as feedback and adjusts its leverage weighting to match what traders are actually doing right now. You see the clusters forming before the sweep happens.

## How it works

The algorithm runs three steps continuously:

1. **Infer position entries.** Changes in open interest combined with taker aggression reveal where new leveraged positions opened and in which direction.
2. **Project liquidation prices.** Each inferred entry is projected across the leverage tiers traders actually use. BTC models tiers from 5x up to 250x. ETH goes up to 125x. SOL up to 75x.
3. **Calibrate against reality.** Real forced-liquidation events from Binance, Bybit, and OKX, normalized into one stream, feed back into the model. The leverage weighting shifts toward what the market is actually running.

Currently supported symbols: **BTC**, **ETH**, and **SOL**.

When price approaches a liquidation cluster, you know what is coming. A cascade of forced orders that will push price through that level. You position accordingly.

## What you see

An intensity grid over price and time. Long liquidation clusters sit below price. Short clusters sit above. Brighter means more projected liquidation volume at that level. Hover any cell for exact values.

The live snapshot updates roughly every 5 seconds. History is stored at 1-minute resolution, with about 12 hours hot and a growing long-term archive behind it.

## Zone lifecycle

Liquidation zones are not static pixels. Every zone moves through a lifecycle:

- **Created** when the model first projects liquidations at a level.
- **Reinforced** when more positions stack at the same level. The zone gets brighter.
- **Swept** when price trades through it. Swept zones fade rather than vanish instantly. A single wick does not erase a level that built up over hours.
- **Expired** when a zone sits untouched for hours and the positions behind it have likely closed or moved.

This is how you tell which levels have already been cleared and which are still loaded.

## Validated in live markets

On February 28, during the Iran strike, the algorithm identified exact liquidation clusters hours before 2,623 force orders swept through them. Traders watching the heatmap saw the setup forming well before the cascade hit. That is the difference between predicting and reacting.

## Impact levels

The heatmap shows everything the model projects. Most of it is mist: small, diffuse liquidation volume that paints useful context but is not individually tradeable. Impact levels cut through the mist. They are the discrete levels most likely to actually matter as support or resistance, scored by a dedicated model and drawn as bright bands over the heatmap.

### How a level earns its score

The terminal tracks every significant level from the moment it forms: how large it is, whether real liquidation prints confirm the projection near that price, how it stands out against the background, and how far it sits from price. A model trained on thousands of archived level lifecycles turns those properties into a single **impact score** between 0 and 1: the probability that price reaching this level produces a real reaction, not just a touch.

The score is calibrated against measured outcomes, not vibes. In training, levels at the bottom of the scale produced a meaningful reaction roughly 1 time in 10. Levels near the top reacted roughly 3 times in 4.

### What you see

- **Impact 0.6 and above**: a bright band in the side's color with a near-white core line. These are the levels to plan around.
- **Impact 0.4 to 0.6**: a subtle tinted band. Worth watching, weaker conviction.
- **Below 0.4**: stays in the mist. Context, not a level.

Bands draw as horizontal rays from the moment the level started resting to the live edge of the chart, so the length of a band tells you honestly how long that level has been sitting there. Up to 12 levels per side are scored, refreshed about every 5 seconds.

Hover any band for the full readout: side, price, age, whether real liquidation prints confirm it (dual-source), its modeled size (shown as approximate, because it is a model estimate, not a resting order), the impact score, and a live accuracy line showing how often similar levels were actually reached. That accuracy number comes from the terminal's own running ledger, measured continuously in production.

### Honesty rules

The model scores current levels only, and the terminal refuses to show you stale data:

- If the live feed is more than 30 seconds old, bands are withheld until it recovers.
- After a backend restart, impact levels take a few minutes to reappear while the level tracker warms up.
- Scores above roughly 0.7 should be read as "very likely to be tested," not strictly ranked against each other. Ultra-close levels almost always get touched but can coin-flip the reaction, so the terminal deliberately avoids showing a ranked leaderboard.

<Note>
Impact bands have a per-pane toggle, **Impact S/R levels (model)**, in the liquidation heatmap settings. It is on by default. Custom indicators can read the same levels through `ctx.liq_impact_levels()`, see the [Lua Scripting Reference](/docs/indicators/scripting-reference).
</Note>

## Additional liquidation tools

The heatmap is the flagship, but the liquidation suite includes more:

- **Historical liquidation heatmap.** Look back at where liquidations occurred on past price action. Study how clusters formed and resolved.
- **Liquidation bubbles overlay.** Plots liquidation events directly on your candlestick chart. See exactly when and where force orders fired.
- **Deduplicated liquidation stream.** Events from Binance, Bybit, and OKX, normalized into one clean feed. No double-counting across exchanges.

## Why this matters

Liquidation cascades are the highest-impact events in crypto markets. They move price faster and further than organic order flow. Knowing where they sit before they fire gives you a structural edge that no amount of technical analysis can replicate.
