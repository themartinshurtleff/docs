# Layouts and Charts

TradeNet Terminal lets you build your workspace the way you want it. Multiple panes, popout windows, saved layouts. Here is how to set it up.

## Layout Templates

Start from a template. Six are built in:

- **1 Chart.** A single full-size pane.
- **2 Columns.** Two panes side by side.
- **2 Rows.** Two panes stacked.
- **Focus + 2.** One large main pane with two smaller panes beside it.
- **4 Grid.** Four equal panes in a 2x2 grid.
- **Flow Desk.** A full order flow workstation with chart, DOM, and tape arranged for execution.

Templates are starting points. Resize and swap panes from there.

## Pane Types

Each pane in a layout can be one of:

- **Chart.** Candlestick, footprint, heatmap, or comparison.
- **DOM ladder.** Depth of market with live order book levels.
- **Time & Sales.** The tape. Real-time prints.
- **Screener.** Scan symbols across exchanges.

Each chart pane has its own symbol, timeframe, indicators, and drawings. Settings are per pane. Two charts in the same layout can look and behave completely differently.

Every pane renders on the GPU independently. Adding panes does not degrade performance the way browser-based terminals do.

## Arranging Your Workspace

- **Resize.** Drag the dividers between panes.
- **Fullscreen.** Expand any pane to fill the window. Drop back to the layout when you are done.
- **Pop out.** Move a pane into its own window and drag it to another monitor. Popout windows stay live and connected to the session.

## Link Groups

Assign panes to a link group and they switch together. Change the symbol or timeframe in one pane, and every pane in the same group follows. Use this to keep a candlestick chart, footprint, and DOM locked to the same market while you rotate through symbols.

Panes outside the group stay independent. Mix linked and unlinked panes freely.

## The Bottom Dock

The Order Management Center lives in a dock at the bottom of the terminal. It is independent of layouts. Switch layouts and it stays put, tracking your orders and positions. See [Order Management](/docs/trading/order-management).

## Managing Layouts

Layouts persist automatically between sessions. Close the terminal, reopen it, and your workspace is exactly where you left it.

The layout manager handles the rest:

- **Add** a new layout from any template.
- **Rename** layouts. "BTC Scalping" is better than "Layout 1".
- **Clone** a layout to use as a base for a variant.
- **Delete** what you no longer use.

<Note>
The Free tier is limited to 1 chart pane and BTC. Verified and paid tiers unlock multi-pane layouts and all symbols.
</Note>

## Common Setups

- **Heatmap + candles.** Heatmap chart on the left, candlestick on the right, same link group. The heatmap shows you the target. The candles show you the price action getting there.
- **Execution desk.** The Flow Desk template: chart, DOM ladder, and tape on one symbol. Full context for entries.
- **Multi-symbol.** BTC, ETH, and SOL charts in a 4 Grid. Watch correlations and divergences in real time.
- **Multi-timeframe.** Same symbol on 1m, 5m, and 1h in unlinked panes. Drill down from macro to micro without switching layouts.

## Tips

- Set up one layout for each strategy you trade. Keep them focused. A scalping layout does not need weekly charts.
- Put the heatmap where you can always see it. It is the highest-signal tool in the terminal.
- Pop the DOM out to a second monitor if you trade the ladder. Full height matters.
- Clone before you experiment. It is faster than rebuilding a layout you broke.
- Name your layouts clearly. You will build more of them than you expect.
