The basics. Every order here is a standard exchange order on Bitunix Futures. For algos like TWAP and icebergs, see [Advanced Orders](/docs/trading/advanced-orders).

## Market order

Executes immediately at the best available price. You pay taker fees and accept some slippage in exchange for certainty.

- **Slippage tolerance (optional).** Cap how far from the current price you are willing to fill. If the book is too thin, the order rejects instead of filling badly.
- **Reduce-only.** Restricts the order to shrinking your position. It can never open or flip you.

Use it when getting in or out matters more than the price you get.

## Limit order

Rests on the book at your price. Fills only at that price or better.

- **Drag to reprice.** Grab the order line on the chart and drag it. Done.
- **Click to edit.** Price and size are editable inline in the trade panel and the Open Orders tab.
- **Reduce-only.** Same as above, the order can only shrink your position.

### Time-in-force

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **GTC** | Good till canceled. Rests until filled or you cancel it. The default. |
| **IOC** | Immediate or cancel. Fills what it can right now, cancels the rest. |
| **FOK** | Fill or kill. Fills the entire size immediately or cancels entirely. |
| **Post-Only** | Rests as a maker order or rejects. Never crosses the spread. |

## Take profit and stop loss

Attach a TP and an SL when you place an order. Bitunix supports one TP and one SL per order.

- Set each as an **absolute price** or a **relative %** from entry.
- Edit later from the positions panel, or drag the TP/SL lines on the chart.
- Each leg can execute as **market** (guaranteed exit, slippage possible) or **limit** (price control, fill not guaranteed).

<Tip>
Stop as market, take profit as limit is a common setup. You want out of a losing trade for sure, and you can afford to be picky on the winner.
</Tip>

## Post-only and reduce-only, plainly

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Post-only">
    Maker only. The order must rest on the book. If it would cross the spread and fill as a taker, the exchange rejects it instead. Use it when you want maker fees, period.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Reduce-only">
    The order can only shrink your position. It will never increase it or flip you to the other side. Use it on every exit so a stale order can't accidentally open a new position.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Which order should I use?

- **Need in or out right now?** Market, with a slippage tolerance if the book is thin.
- **Have a price in mind and can wait?** Limit, GTC.
- **Want maker fees guaranteed?** Limit with Post-Only.
- **Testing for immediate liquidity without resting?** IOC or FOK.
- **Closing a position?** Always flag it reduce-only.
- **Big size, or want to hide it?** That's [Advanced Orders](/docs/trading/advanced-orders) territory.

## Closing positions

Three ways out, all in the positions panel:

- **Flash close.** One action, instant full market close of the position.
- **Partial close.** Choose an amount, close just that much.
- **Close all positions.** Closes everything, with a confirmation step first.

<Warning>
Flash close is a market order. In thin or fast markets, expect slippage. If you need a specific exit price, use a reduce-only limit instead.
</Warning>
