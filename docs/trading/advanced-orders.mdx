Advanced orders are execution algorithms. You define a strategy, the terminal works it for you: slicing size, walking the book, refilling hidden orders.

## How they run: the client-side model

Be clear about this before using them. Advanced orders run **in your terminal, not on the exchange**.

- The algo only runs while the terminal is open. Close the terminal, the algo stops.
- Any resting child orders already on the exchange stay there as plain limit or stop orders. The exchange doesn't know they belong to an algo.

### Exit policy

Every advanced order has an Exit Policy, chosen at placement:

| Policy | On graceful quit |
| --- | --- |
| **Close on terminal exit** (default) | All child orders are canceled. Clean slate. |
| **Keep on terminal exit** | Child orders rest on the exchange as ordinary orders. |

If the terminal crashes instead of quitting cleanly, a recovery dialog appears on next launch. It shows what was running and what's still resting on the exchange, and lets you resume, adopt, or cancel.

<Warning>
With "Keep on terminal exit", resting children have no algo managing them. A half-placed ladder or a paused TWAP remainder will just sit there. Know what you're leaving behind.
</Warning>

### Limits and safety rails

- Max **5 concurrent algos**, max **100 child orders** per algo.
- Before submit, a confirmation modal shows a locked snapshot of the full strategy. What you confirm is what runs.
- **Paper mode** works with every advanced order type. Use it.

## Scaled (Limit Scale)

Places N limit orders across a price range in one action. A ladder, built for you.

**Parameters**

- Side, total size
- Price range (low and high)
- Number of orders: 2 to 100
- Price distribution: **Even**, **Cubic**, or **Exponential**, with a skew slider that biases rungs toward one end of the range
- Size distribution: flat or skewed, independent from price distribution. Weight size toward the far end, the near end, or keep it equal
- Variance (optional): randomizes each order's price and size by up to ±10%, so your ladder doesn't leave an obvious footprint on the book
- Post-only, reduce-only

**Behavior**

Orders go out in batches to respect exchange rate limits. A 20-rung ladder takes a few seconds to place. Sizes are rounded to the instrument's precision; dust below the minimum order size is merged into the next rung.

Scaled is fire-and-forget. Once placed, the rungs are independent limit orders. The terminal doesn't need to stay open for them to fill. You can cancel them as a group.

**When to use it**

- Laddering into a position across a support or resistance zone
- Scaling out into strength instead of picking one exit
- Building size without showing one large order

## TWAP

Time-weighted average price. Splits a total size into slices executed at intervals over a set duration.

**Parameters**

- Side, total size
- Duration: 5 minutes to 12 hours
- Slice count, or interval: 5 to 120 seconds
- Timing jitter: randomizes each interval by up to ±20%
- Irregular lots: randomizes each slice size by up to 20%
- Child order type: market for now, chase children optional (see [TWAP with chase children](#twap-with-chase-children))
- Price bounds, with a reaction setting for when price leaves them:
  - **Pause** (default): waits until price re-enters the bounds, then resumes
  - **Abort**: stops the algo
  - **Execute Remainder**: markets the rest immediately
- Reduce-only, exit policy

**Behavior**

Failed or skipped slices roll forward into the next slice, so the total still gets done. At the end of the duration, any remainder is handled per your completion setting.

**When to use it**

- Entering or exiting size over time with less market impact
- Averaging in around a level you expect price to hang near

## Chase Limit

A limit order that automatically follows the best bid or ask. It uses order modify, not cancel and replace, so it keeps queue position cleanly and doesn't spam the book.

**Parameters**

- Side, size
- Peg: the best price on your side of the book
- Max chase distance (required): in % or ticks. How far price can run against you before the chase gives up
- Mode: **Maintain** (follows price in both directions) or **Trail** (only follows toward the market, like a trailing entry)
- Lifetime: default 2 minutes
- Give-up action: **Cancel**, **Keep as resting limit**, or **Cross to market**

**Behavior**

Repricing is event-driven off the top of book, not on a timer. Orders are post-only by default; if a reprice gets rejected for crossing, it retries immediately.

**When to use it**

- Getting filled passively without paying taker fees
- Entries where you want the maker rebate side
- Chasing thin books where a market order would slip badly

## Iceberg

Shows only a small visible portion of your order. As each visible tranche fills, the next one is placed automatically.

**Parameters**

- Side, total size
- Visible size: how much shows on the book at once
- Tranche randomization: each refill is 50 to 100% of the visible size, so the refill pattern is harder to read
- Price: fixed limit for now
- Price guard: pauses refills if price moves away from your level
- Reduce-only

**Behavior**

Refills trigger off fill events, typically within 100 to 300 ms. Partial fills leave the resting order untouched, so you keep your queue priority.

**When to use it**

- Building or unwinding size at a level without showing your hand

---

## TWAP with chase children

TWAP where each slice is a chase limit instead of a market order. Each slice chases for its interval. On timeout, it either markets out or rolls into the next slice, per your setting.

**When to use it**

- TWAP execution while staying on the maker side as much as possible. Slower fills, better fees.

---

## Swarm

Fires N market orders concurrently, as fast as exchange rate limits allow.

**Parameters**

- Side, total size
- Lot count
- Irregular sizes toggle: randomizes lot sizes
- Reduce-only

**When to use it**

- Instant size when speed matters more than fees. News, breakouts, emergency exits.

## Position automation

<Note>
**Rolling out.** These features are shipping gradually and may not be available on your account yet.
</Note>

Automation that manages your stop after entry:

- **Stop to breakeven.** When your TP fills or a trigger price crosses, the SL automatically moves to entry plus fees. The trade can no longer lose.
- **Trailing stop.** The SL ratchets behind the high-water mark of mark price. It only moves in your favor, never back.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if I close the terminal?">
    The algo stops. With "Close on terminal exit" (the default), child orders are canceled on a graceful quit. With "Keep on terminal exit", resting children stay on the exchange as plain orders, unmanaged.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens if the terminal crashes?">
    Resting child orders stay on the exchange. On next launch, a recovery dialog shows what was running and lets you resume, adopt the orphaned orders, or cancel them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why did my ladder place only some orders?">
    Orders go out in batches against exchange rate limits, and individual rungs can be rejected (insufficient margin, price filters, post-only crossing). On partial placement the terminal reports "placed k of N" and offers a group cancel so you're not left with half a ladder you didn't want.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do advanced orders work with TP/SL?">
    Yes. The same TP/SL rules from [Order Types](/docs/trading/order-types#take-profit-and-stop-loss) apply: one TP and one SL, absolute or relative, market or limit execution.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is there a limit?">
    5 concurrent algos, 100 child orders per algo.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
