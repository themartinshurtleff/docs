# Liquidation Heatmap

The liquidation heatmap shows modeled liquidation density across price and time for aggregated BTC, ETH, and SOL.

It is a model. It is not a map of exact exchange-held positions.

## Turn it on

Select a chart pane and choose **Liquidation** from the heatmap control in the selected-pane toolbar.

Open the heatmap settings to configure:

- Theme
- Automatic, SD, or HD resolution
- Intensity
- Opacity
- Minimum notional
- Model impact bands

The chart automatically chooses historical heatmap products that match the visible timeframe and scale.

## Reading the grid

The heatmap is a price-time surface.

- Brighter areas indicate greater modeled liquidation density
- Long-liquidation structure is below the relevant market price
- Short-liquidation structure is above it
- Persistent horizontal areas show levels that have remained active through time

Hover the chart for the available value and metadata. Model-derived values are shown as approximate.

## Historical and live data

TradeNet loads binary heatmap history in tiles, then continues with live data.

The loader tracks actual frame coverage. If a tile range contains an interior gap, the terminal can request that missing area instead of marking the entire envelope complete.

## Model impact levels

Impact levels are a filtered view of current liquidation structure. They highlight modeled levels that are more likely to matter if price reaches them.

Each level has an impact score from 0 to 1. The score considers the live model state and observed liquidation behavior. It is a probability-like model output, not certainty.

The bands:

- Begin where the tracked level formed
- Extend toward the live edge
- Show how long the level has persisted
- Can include observed liquidation confirmation
- Hide when the live source is stale

The model bands toggle is available in liquidation heatmap settings.

## Warmup and staleness

Impact levels are live-only.

After a service restart, they need time to rebuild enough live state. They can disappear temporarily during warmup or when the feed is stale. That is intentional.

<Warning>
Do not read impact score as a guaranteed reaction or as a strict ranking between very close levels. A level can be reached, swept, ignored, or traded through.
</Warning>

## Combine it with other tools

The heatmap becomes more useful when compared with:

- Orderbook depth
- Liquidation events
- Open-interest changes
- Footprint aggression
- Volume and OI CVD
