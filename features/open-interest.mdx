# Open Interest and Net Positioning

Open interest helps separate new risk entering the market from existing risk leaving it.

TradeNet provides three direct OI metrics and one modeled positioning indicator.

## Open Interest

The Open Interest row shows the level of outstanding derivatives exposure.

The backend retains OI level in base-asset units. The chart display converts it to USD with the matching candle close so the plotted level is easier to compare with USD OI Delta.

## OI Delta

OI Delta is the change in open interest during the candle, shown in USD.

General interpretations:

- Price up, OI up: new exposure is entering during the rise
- Price down, OI up: new exposure is entering during the decline
- Price up, OI down: positions are closing into the rise
- Price down, OI down: positions are closing into the decline

These are descriptions, not automatic long or short labels.

## Daily OI CVD

Daily OI CVD accumulates OI Delta and resets at UTC midnight.

It shows whether the session has added or removed net open interest over time. It is not a rolling 24-hour metric.

## Venue selection

OI metrics can use supported subsets of Binance, Bybit, and OKX on aggregated markets.

Keep the selected venue set consistent when comparing OI with other aggregated metrics.

## Net Positioning

Net Positioning estimates how OI change may be distributed between long and short positioning using:

- Aggressor flow
- Price pressure
- OI change
- Configurable smoothing and flow weighting

The indicator preserves the OI-change total while allocating modeled long and short components.

It is not exchange-reported long and short account positioning.

## Practical reading

Use OI with price, delta, and liquidations.

For example, a sharp move down with a large negative OI Delta and long-liquidation events is better described as an OI flush or OI contraction than as an exact count of liquidated longs.

<Note>
OI history begins when the backend has retained samples. After a service restart, the newest hot history must rebuild. Missing OI is shown as missing, not zero.
</Note>
