# Using the Liquidation Heatmap

The heatmap is context. It tells you where modeled forced flow may become relevant. It does not tell you exactly when to enter.

## Start with structure

Zoom out enough to see:

- The nearest meaningful cluster above price
- The nearest meaningful cluster below price
- Which side is denser
- Which levels have persisted
- Whether current price is moving toward or away from them

Do not begin by staring at the brightest single pixel.

## Check whether the level is current

Before using an impact band:

- Confirm the feed is healthy
- Confirm the band is not withheld as stale
- Allow warmup time after a restart
- Read modeled size as approximate

## Compare effort and result

Use footprint and CVD to ask whether aggressive flow is producing price movement.

Examples:

- Strong aggressive buying with little upward progress can mean absorption
- Falling price with contracting OI can mean positions are closing instead of fresh shorts entering
- Rising OI into a nearby downside liquidation area can add fuel, but direction still needs confirmation

## Compare the orderbook

An orderbook heatmap or Orderbook pane shows resting liquidity.

A liquidation level aligned with thin depth may allow faster movement. A large resting wall can absorb, slow, or redirect the move. It can also be pulled.

## Watch actual liquidation events

Liquidation bubbles show observed events. They help answer whether projected structure is beginning to resolve into real forced orders.

Remember that not every venue reports the exact execution price. Read [Liquidation Events](/features/liquidation-events) before treating every bubble as an exact tick.

## Build a trade plan

A basic plan should define:

1. The level that matters
2. The condition that confirms your idea
3. The condition that invalidates it
4. The stop location
5. The target and expected path

The heatmap gives you candidate structure. The rest of the terminal helps determine whether the market is actually trading the way your plan requires.

<Tip>
Paper trade the same setup repeatedly before assigning money to it. Save screenshots and note what OI, delta, CVD, footprint, and resting liquidity looked like before the level was reached.
</Tip>
